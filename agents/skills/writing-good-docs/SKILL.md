## Plain English

Write utilitarian prose in simple English and simple grammar. Prefer common
words and concrete statements. Use technical terminology only when it is the
project's precise term.

Keep the tone neutral and factual. No startup or marketing voice, hype, praise,
or sales language. Document what exists, what it does, and what the reader must
know.

## Say it once

Avoid repetition and filler. Add a sentence only when it carries new
information. Cut praise, obvious statements, and restatements.

## Assume competence

The reader understands programming and the project's tools. Skip beginner
explanations and hand-holding. State constraints, decisions, and behavior
directly.

## Read it through

When modifying documentation, read the changed section locally and the whole
document globally. Check that concepts are introduced before they are used, and
that the document reads cleanly from start to end. A reader should not need to
jump back and forth to understand it.
